# Learning what evidence to obtain next

28 September 2026. Bounded root methods, artifact and theory work, authorized
while evidence-set memory proceeds independently. The
[reading and inspection ledger](../sources/2026-09-28-evidence-acquisition/README.md)
records P110–P113, exact revisions, replay and limits.

**Conclusion:** useful investigation can depend on both retained answers and
retained ways of obtaining evidence. The inspected sources make this distinction
concrete, but do not establish a learning advantage for a general ask/search/test
chooser. Deprioritize a separate action-classification study. Retain the narrower
question of whether experience improves construction of decision-relevant checks
on later unfamiliar work, with prior answers and ordinary investigation available.
This is a research preference, not a new commission or an amendment to an active
study's methods.

## What changed since the observation-boundaries analysis

The [earlier analysis](OBSERVATION_BOUNDARIES.md) showed why repeatedly executing
an implementation cannot always identify its intended contract. The present
inspection advances that argument in two ways. A real maintenance history
separates establishing a compatibility requirement from checking its realization.
An elicited dialogue release shows that asking can narrow scope while several
responses remain reasonable, and may still leave the original uncertainty open.

The public-method comparison also substantially narrows generic proposals.
Learned retrieval has a direct trained precedent (P111), costly context choice
has an explicit decision-theoretic treatment (P112), and choosing among memory,
verification and clarification already has a dedicated benchmark (P113). P110
provides inspectable human source-seeking decisions. Reproducing one of these
mechanisms can be useful; claiming novelty or continuing memory value requires
a more specific comparison.

## Grounded cases and their implications

The [case record](../sources/2026-09-28-evidence-acquisition/CASES.md) inspects one
Requests issue/repair lineage and three decisions from two InSCIt conversations.
They were chosen for diagnosis, not to estimate a population effect.

In Requests, the decision to preserve existing exception-catching behavior is
retained in discussion and documentation. Later malformed inputs expose a
decoding path that fails to implement it. Root replayed the exact follow-up base
and merged source: two inferred-encoding failure cases escape the wrapper before
the repair and satisfy it afterward; three valid controls remain unchanged.
These component checks confirm the mechanism, not an agent's diagnostic skill.
They also expose why the final implementation must be read: an earlier PR's body
describes a backend replacement that the reviewed merged code does not perform.

The relevant reuse is conditional. Retaining the requirement avoids reopening
an already resolved policy. Retaining a test can check the same behavior again.
A more general lesson about inspecting alternate decoding paths might guide a
different repair, but that transfer has not been tested. A model that merely
selects a tool named “test” has not yet selected a distinguishing input.

The InSCIt audit finds 77 of 502 development turns with retained references of
different response types. A selected conversation first asks the user to choose
a category and receives that preference. On the next turn, the release accepts
both a direct answer and further narrowing. Another conversation narrows a
historical topic without establishing the originally requested earliest date.
Thus a reply to a question, agreement with an action label, and completion of the
task are different observations. The recorded continuation belongs to one branch;
it cannot serve as the response to every new question an agent might ask.

## The unit to learn is a consequential decision

For an unresolved decision, identify the information already available, the
competing actions, and which possible observation would change the action or its
expected outcome. This extends the existing
[decision-value account](MAINTENANCE_DECISIONS.md#price-information-about-actions-including-the-alternatives).
P112 supplies a close public formulation; this is not a new acquisition objective.

Three distinctions matter for future comparisons:

1. **Answer reuse.** A retained contract, preference or prior result resolves the
   same uncertainty while its scope and authority remain valid. Buying it again
   is not a competent memoryless comparator when ordinary storage is allowed.
2. **Investigation reuse.** Earlier work helps identify a useful source, formulate
   a discriminating query/input, or recognize a relevant observation on a new
   problem. This can help even when the earlier answer no longer applies.
3. **Decision and completion.** The acquired observation changes what the agent
   does, and the resulting complete work improves or avoids cost. Choosing a
   plausible source without using its answer establishes neither benefit.

A source's role is conditional on the question. Documentation can already record
an authoritative requirement; testing can reveal an external service's behavior;
a responsible person can resolve intent when that is what remains unknown.
Consequently, fixed labels such as “world question” or “user question” do not
replace an assessment of what a particular observation can settle. Competent
ordinary review should exploit this structure before a learned chooser is credited.

## Consequences for where experience lives

| Retained experience | Plausible role | What would establish added value |
|---|---|---|
| Source answer, scope and revision | Explicit record for a resolved requirement or fact | Avoids repeat inquiry while remaining applicable |
| A concrete regression check or evidence-gathering procedure | Executable artifact | Produces relevant observations reliably and cheaply on later use |
| A transferable strategy for constructing/checking observations | Contextual lesson, specialist weights, or both | Helps fresh complete work beyond the same available records and tools |
| Permission or ownership boundary | Declared runtime rule and applicable source | Enforces authorization; task success does not itself supply permission |

These are conditional allocation hypotheses, not a one-to-one taxonomy or an
empirical ranking of substrates. An executable check is also acquired experience.
A useful model may learn to construct it, and explicit records can preserve the
authority and failure history needed to revise it.

## Research selection and phase closure

The evidence **answers** the generic availability of trained retrieval and
source-choice formulations, **narrows** action-label benchmarks as evidence for
continuing capability, and **redirects** local attention toward construction and
use of distinguishing observations. More numerical variants of an ask/verify
classifier would currently have lower scientific value than resolving that step.

A meaningful later contrast would retain equal eligible histories, code, tests,
prior source answers and normal tools, then vary the additional acquired strategy
on fresh problems whose answers were unavailable during acquisition. It should
compare against a developed ordinary investigation policy and comparable extra
current-task effort. Outcomes should cover complete new and still-valid work;
costs include unsuccessful queries, source preparation, training, human or
simulated replies, reading and repair. The study investigator would choose the
smallest workable implementation, rather than inherit a root-designed protocol.

The present histories do not supply all counterfactual observations, calibrated
action values or a cross-session learner comparison. Those absences limit claims;
they do not require retiring learning or waiting for a naturally perfect dataset.
Motivated construction and bounded acquisition diagnosis remain legitimate, with
their authored parts explicit and fresh confirmation after development.

to deprioritize a standalone classifier commission. The broader evidence-choice
