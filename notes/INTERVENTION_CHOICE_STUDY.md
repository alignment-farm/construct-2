# Does experience choosing interventions survive an agent change?

28 September 2026. The user approved continuing the
[adaptation-selection recommendation](ADAPTATION_SELECTION.md). This commissions
**intervention-choice-transfer**, an independent bounded feasibility investigation.
The root owns the question and synthesis; the ancillary investigator owns workload
discovery, methods, diagnostic development, execution and publication.

> Can outcomes from earlier interventions improve adaptation decisions on fresh
> work after the receiving agent or interface changes, beyond competent current
> diagnosis and unchanged reuse of the old decision policy?

This develops AD1–AD2 without changing their original wording. It is distinct
from learning to investigate within a task and from evidence-set assembly. Those
studies retain their existing remits; this investigation need not wait for them.

## Why this comparison

The [preceding reading](../sources/2026-09-28-adaptation-selection/README.md)
already identifies direct public allocation precedents. Demonstrating that model
and harness changes can jointly help is insufficient. We want to know what useful
decision experience persists, what receiving-state changes invalidate it, and
when reacquiring decision evidence is worth doing.

Keep three contributions identifiable: better candidate remedies, better choices
among available remedies, and better complete subsequent work. An acquired policy
may live in explicit records, contextual instructions, executable rules or
weights. Neural training is optional. An action may be a short sequence where
the components complement one another; a competition among all five substrates
is unnecessary.

## Initial expectation

Begin with a tractable task family, a small set of credible remedies and one
material change to the recipient or interface. Establish that the remedies can
be applied and that their consequences can be judged through complete outcomes,
including still-valid obligations. The investigator may use published components
or motivated authored tasks, with their provenance and limitations explicit.
Do not presume that a useful choice problem exists merely because failures have
different labels or different remedies produce different scores.

The first phase includes discovering whether earlier outcomes contain useful
information beyond the present-state observations. A developed diagnostic rule,
unchanged reuse of the earlier policy, and a good fixed remedy or sequence are
serious alternatives. Source evidence, ordinary retrieval, caching and repair
should remain available. Separate inherited investigator/public knowledge from
feedback actually earned by the experimental decision maker. New task wording
alone does not establish transfer across receiving states or histories.

If the comparison supports acquisition, pursue bounded learning and diagnosis;
do not stop after a minimally developed failed recipe. If ordinary reuse or a
cheap current-state rule explains the available outcomes, publish that boundary.
Assess claims developed through exploration on fresh material. Charge discovery,
trial interventions, failed attempts, policy acquisition, observations, later use
and maintenance in relevant native units; a perfect retrospective choice is a
diagnostic bound, not a deployable free policy.

These are scientific distinctions rather than a prescribed factorial design.
The study selects informative contrasts and can refine the question as evidence
develops. Phase closure rests on explanatory progress, a demonstrated limitation
or a concrete resource constraint; a learned-policy advantage is not required.

## Assessment after the first publication — 29 September

The [root review](../studies/2026-09-29-intervention-choice-findings.md) advances
the preparation boundary to `cb25d5c64bd64859e0e9abbcfc41f0e8f44762df` and accepts
the first bounded experiment plus source-parser follow-up. Counting 24 earlier
old-interface outcomes produces a functioning explicit category policy. On eighteen
fresh nested-API tasks it completes sixteen, versus seventeen for a fixed canonical
adapter; shared source-order repair brings a tie. The global inherited choice
already matches ordinary reuse. This is an interface change with the model fixed,
not receiver-model transfer or an isolated causal estimate of chooser degradation.

The first action pair is complementary, but its 18/18 retrospective bound requires
outcome knowledge unavailable to a deployed chooser. A subsequently developed and
frozen parser handles the closed source grammar, completing 36/36 fresh API tasks
(eighteen source cases, two interfaces) without model calls. Its development is
charged as remedy construction, not learned decision improvement. Freshness means
new outcome evaluation within the same grammar; earlier structural tests had
instantiated the seeds, as disclosed. Root replays saved generations, decisions,
repairs and parser outputs and verifies historical freeze hashes.

This establishes no added intervention-choice value or repayment of acquisition.
It is a useful explained workload limit, not a general failure of learning. Close
this template phase and retain the broader remit. A later workload should first
establish reliable source labels and what remains unresolved after competent
ordinary parsing, retrieval and repair; then assess whether observable information
or earlier outcomes can improve the decisions. Linguistic variety alone is not
sufficient, and a positive selector gap is not an admission requirement. P126–P127
narrow the relevant method comparison. The investigator owns further methods and
workload discovery; this review makes no new commission.
