Several weaknesses materially constrain interpretation. The acquisition comparison uses one model, one seed, one adapter recipe, and a small synthetic procedure. Its 64 inputs contain sixteen identifier clusters. The withheld combination depends on an assumed separable rule that the demonstrations do not uniquely specify. Conversely, the perfect supplied-rule control receives privileged information: it diagnoses execution ability without proving acquisition from equal evidence. Representation and construction method also remain entangled—a failed lesson writer and a fixed supervised adapter cannot establish general limits of textual or parametric memory.
