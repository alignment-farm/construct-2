# Is observing the learner worth more than knowing its history?

16 September 2026. Root preparation requested after the
[maintenance-decision development](MAINTENANCE_DECISIONS.md). This is a concrete
research choice and proposed bounded scope, not a frozen experimental protocol
or a fifth S2 commission. The [preparation ledger](../sources/2026-09-16-maintenance-preparation/README.md)
records the evidence, source revisions and review limits.

**Recommend a bounded test of history-rule transfer before investing in a learned
maintenance selector.** The original S2 workload supplies functioning acquisition
and revision actions, but its published support reversal is entirely compatible
with a simple rule using known history. A comparison can usefully establish that
this rule transfers, or identify when a current-state observation improves on it.
Neither outcome requires a neural selector advantage. A broad placement or
multi-intervention controller campaign is not justified by this preparation.

## The strongest available explanation is cheaper than a state predictor

The reviewed S2 head remains `3e71eb5f146e6493c60cef26d15d86dedd2249fb`, identical
to the root's recorded boundary and current remote head. Reading its task and
revision implementation confirms a small finite work-order problem: certification
and two scoped waivers determine eligibility; stock determines reservation,
shipment and final status. Sixteen condition combinations occur across identities.
Evaluation removes policy instructions and requires a complete five-field action.

The two current support choices are already functioning learning interventions:
every published endpoint acquires the newest waiver. Their differing preservation
is the object to predict. A new method need not first invent an effective editor.

The root recomputed all 2,304 saved endpoint answers using the published task
rules. Equal weighting of the six published starting states gives:

| Retrospective choice | Complete orders across six 192-case suites |
|---|---:|
| Always Novel support | 888 / 1,152 |
| Always Bridged support | 825 / 1,152 |
| Match support to the recorded revision-1 history | 912 / 1,152 |
| Choose the better aggregate endpoint for each state after seeing results | 912 / 1,152 |

The match-history rule chooses Novel after Novel history and Bridged after
Bridged history. It ties both supports in two states and selects the better one
in the remaining four. Its zero aggregate regret is **post hoc**, based on three
acquisitions with related descendants, including selected diagnostic material.
It is neither a trained selector nor a held-out transfer result. All this material
is now development evidence. The 24-order gap above the best constant action
cannot be advertised as headroom beyond known history. The policy also leaves
240 failures, so oracle matching is far from complete maintenance.

This changes the investment: test whether known history remains sufficient
before interpreting a state-dependent outcome as a need for paid state sensing.

## Workload choice and alternatives

| Candidate | What makes it useful | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| S2 work orders, newly acquired learners | Demonstrated complete acquisition, exact state consequences, functioning support alternatives and known history controls | Preferred for a bounded decision-transfer comparison; reuse code and task semantics with provenance in an independent study |
| P27's released task-forgetting matrices | Useful public check of matrix-completion implementation without rerunning large LLMs | Method calibration only: rows are separate task fine-tunes, not independently acquired and revised histories with alternative support outcomes |
| A new operational document/agent workload | Potentially broader practical maintenance and placement relevance | Defer here: functioning neural acquisition and useful alternative updates are not yet established; recreating them would change this into another acquisition study |

Reuse of the S2 task is justified by the changed question, not its recency.
The bounded claim would remain within this model/task family. Separately acquired
weights and histories matter; new entity names alone do not test decision transfer.

The initial action set is Novel support, Bridged support, and explicit execution
from retained evidence. Include no revision as a reference, charging its unmet
new obligations. The published formal-policy interpreter is an explicitly
privileged reference: it is handed executable authoritative rules. It cannot
establish an equal-information placement advantage. A deployable explicit control
should use the same checked examples and corrections available to learning,
retain current labels, and expose its addressing assumptions. The sixteen-condition
structure makes a compact example table a serious candidate, not a weak prompt
control. If it dominates, retain that conclusion; withholding evidence or adding
irrelevant difficulty to force repayment would not improve the comparison.

## Decisions and observations to compare

The minimum comparison includes the best development-selected constant support,
the explicit match-history rule, and a developed rule using history metadata
such as earlier support and acquisition duration. None may use a seed identifier
as a proxy for held-out outcomes. Then ask whether observations add value:

| Method | Information acquired at decision time | What it can save |
|---|---|---|
| Frozen history-conditioned action prediction | Recorded history, fitted on other acquisition roots | Both trial training and validation; no current-state benefit is claimed |
| Pre-update observation | A small fixed panel of complete answers or target losses under the current specification | Potentially all trial training; effectiveness is unknown |
| Prefix-trial prediction | Restore each candidate from the same state; observe after a short matched training prefix | Potentially avoids completing an unpromising candidate |
| Sparse direct validation | Complete both candidates, then score a small obligation-covering panel | Saves evaluation relative to full validation, but does not save candidate training |
| Full direct validation | Complete both candidates and evaluate a dedicated validation suite | Strong paid alternative to prediction; final evaluation stays separate |

A starting panel can cover all sixteen condition combinations on a development
identity, with a second identity testing entity sensitivity. It must include new
and earlier waivers, still-forbidden cases, stock and action completion. Coverage
of these symbolic conditions does not guarantee coverage of the neural learner's
failures. Define the panel before fresh outcomes, and never reuse it as the final
evaluation suite. Loss measurements require valid targets; labels and their
source are part of the observation's access and cost.

P27's actual author release supplies additive, KNN and factorized completion of
a held-out row from measured entries. Begin with simple history-conditioned and
additive/KNN comparators before a higher-capacity fit. An endpoint completion
model is not automatically an early-training extrapolator: prefix-to-endpoint
training pairs require their own calibration. Group all descendants of one
acquisition together in data splits. Known tasks with new roots test acquisition
transfer; a separately declared revision-order change tests another boundary.
Do not combine both into one undifferentiated generalization score.

## Bounded sizing and accounting

An illustrative scope is four new acquisition roots for development and four
untouched roots for assessment, each producing the two known revision-1 histories
and two revision-2 candidates per history. Eight roots are a small controlled
comparison, not a broad reliability estimate. Preserve unsuccessful acquisition
ladders and imperfect histories; do not replace roots to manufacture useful
selection. A later investigator owns counts and may choose a smaller explanatory
contrast or revise the workload with an explicit rationale.

Using the published schedules, this illustration costs 9,216 revision updates
plus acquisition: 13,312 total if all eight acquire at 512 steps, or 17,408 at
1,024. These counts exclude additional diagnostic recipes, checks and explicit
control development. They are planning arithmetic, not a model-run authorization
or a timing forecast. The prior study reports a peak MLX allocation of about
9.6 GB; current model files, gradients, saved optimizer restoration and shared
hardware availability still require local verification. Serving alone supplies
no gradient access. Public P27's multi-GPU training setup is not the proposed
local resource requirement.

For one decision with two 192-update candidates and a 24-update trial prefix,
prefix selection could use `2*24 + (192-24) = 216` updates if the selected
weights and optimizer are retained exactly. Restarting it instead costs 240;
full candidate validation uses 384, while a fixed choice uses 192. This is only
update-count arithmetic. Charge generations, fitting, storage, trial loss
calculations and explicit use separately. A selected full candidate can be kept;
do not charge an unnecessary third training run to direct validation.

Acquiring every endpoint for scientific evaluation is investigation cost even
when a deployed policy would select earlier. Keep both ledgers. Complete task
quality, new acquisition, preserved obligations and repaired old errors are
separate outcomes. Development-fitted forecast error is diagnostic; the main
question is the fresh decision's quality and cost against these controls.

## What would change the root account

If history rules transfer, the existing interaction need not require current-state
observation in this regime. If cheap probes or prefixes improve choices on fresh
roots beyond those rules, they support AD2's observation-value expectation within
the tested scope. If full or sparse direct validation is cheaper at comparable
quality, forecast investment is unjustified there. If all learned choices remain
inadequate and explicit access dominates, selection is not the main limitation.

These are useful outcomes, not admission conditions requiring a predicted win.
Purposeful diagnosis remains appropriate for failed acquisition or a materially
ambiguous result. The proposed stopping point is an explanation of whether known
history or paid observation supports useful choice; it is not a promise to find
a reliable neural controller. AD1–AD3 retain their original wording and status.

The concrete next commission, if selected, should be an independent bounded
history-rule/observation comparison with these controls. S2 stays complete.
This preparation has run no learner, created no new ancillary repository and
dispatched no experimental task.
