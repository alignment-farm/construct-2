**A correction can change a decision without being a learned procedure;
preserving a correct rule can still leave its application unreliable.**
Cycle 2 now has concrete examples of both distinctions, with narrow empirical
scope. Four development worlds do not establish a population effect.

## What the reproduction establishes

The adapted M2 reproduction completed three fixed chains. In all three, the
external correction changed the later resident answer from citing a retracted
finding to declining it; removing the correction reversed that decision.
The stored correction's text matched the earlier M2 evidence exactly. This
supports the original one-case correction mechanism, with recorded inference
adaptations. It does not demonstrate model-generated reflection or transfer
to a different problem.

## What the extension changes

All construction policies received the same scripted, executed source
experience and checked corrections. Fresh task pairs reversed the condition
that determined correct operation order. The relevant unit was a world and
its constructed memories, not each of its repeated actor calls.

| Representation | Successful matched pairs | Composed matched pairs |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| No memory, generic strategy, or raw evidence | 0/16 each | 0/8 each |
| General lessons at either word cap | 0/16 each | 0/8 each |
| Scoped lessons, 48 words | 2/16 | 0/8 |
| Scoped lessons, 144 words | 4/16 | 0/8 |
| Supplied correct conditional rules | 12/16 | 4/8 |

The scoped advantage at 48 words came from one world; at 144 words, from two.
The mean budget interaction was −12.5 percentage points: the conjectured
larger benefit under tighter compression did not appear. These are descriptive
results without confidence intervals or p-values. They do not establish that
conditions generally help, or that more memory generally improves transfer.

The generated text often erased real conditions or assigned the wrong field
to a rule. Four scoped memories were truncated, including one long repetitive
output. Yet the four individual failures with correct supplied rules were all
composed tasks, repeated in two worlds. Consequently, improving construction
alone is not a sufficient explanation of how to close the observed gap.

This sharpens the forest-level question: **which decision distinctions must
survive persistence, and what must the consumer do to use them together?**
Information can be present without being correctly abstracted, and a correct
abstraction can be present without guiding a multi-object plan. The result
connects applicability to placement: the representation's value depends on
both its content and the computation needed to apply it.

## Failure accounting matters to that interpretation

The first pilot stopped after three empty writer responses exhausted their
output allowances. No actor comparison occurred. A documented per-request
template-setting amendment and a fixed six-call workload diagnostic preceded
the fresh successor pilot. All failures and costs are retained separately.
The completed pilot had 16 nonempty writers and 256 nonempty, valid-JSON actor
outputs; its 152 execution failures remain in the reported denominators.
Independent validation and re-execution agreed with all scored outcomes.

This separates an initial inference-configuration limitation from later
participant errors. Neither should be concealed as a missing data point or
inflated into a general theory of memory.
