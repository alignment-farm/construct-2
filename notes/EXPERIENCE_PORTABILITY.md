# What experience remains useful when the agent changes?

17 September 2026. Initially proposed as the next root focus while
executable-experience-retention runs independently, as reported by the user.
Its unpublished work has not been inspected. The initial reasoning is preserved
below; the [subsequent selection](#selection-after-the-methods-review) narrows
this question and records a separate ancillary commission.

The working hypothesis permits the model, harness and retained experience to
develop together. That makes an accumulated memory's value conditional on its
recipient. The root should now ask:

> When the model or harness changes, which acquired experience should carry over
> unchanged, which needs adaptation, and when is rebuilding or retiring it better?

## Why this question now

The active investigation owns retained implementation versus competent
reconstruction. The independent uncertainty here is the receiving system:
experience valuable to one configuration may become redundant, difficult to use
or misleading in another. A possible example is a lesson compensating for an old
model's weakness after that weakness has disappeared. This is a hypothesis to
examine, not an observed local result.

The [adaptation synthesis](ADAPTATION_DECISIONS.md) already motivates compatibility.
AD2 concerned maintenance choices across learner histories; this question concerns
the inherited experience itself. Its bounded assessment and all AD, EU and EX
predictions remain unchanged. No completed phase is reopened.

The [initial reading](../sources/2026-09-17-experience-portability/README.md) finds
direct cross-model transfer and procedural revision precedents. Transfer's mere
possibility and generic automatic rewriting are therefore weak next investments.
The remaining candidate concerns choosing what to carry forward and whether that
choice improves complete behavior enough to justify its work.

## Initial root deliverable

Develop a source-grounded comparison of carrying experience unchanged, selectively
adapting it, reconstructing it from preserved evidence, and using the new system
without the inherited artifact. Separate three explanations:

| Possible explanation | Consequence for the comparison |
|---|---|
| The experience still contains useful information, but its interface no longer fits | Translation or a changed read procedure may suffice. |
| The new model already supplies the capability | Retiring a redundant lesson can be a successful outcome. |
| The experience encodes assumptions that no longer hold | Recovery of the original evidence or reacquisition may matter more than reformatting. |

Start analytically with the task and authoritative facts unchanged, so a changed
recipient is distinguishable from a changed obligation. Assess inherited value
against that recipient's own competent baseline. Better absolute performance
after a model upgrade does not establish a benefit from the inherited memory.
Preserve access to source evidence explicitly; rebuilding from a rich archive
differs from learning again without it. Count inspection, conversion, checking,
use and failures on the relevant sequence. A receiving-system comparison need
not put incompatible weight tensors into another architecture; it can examine
the capability and evidence needed to reconstruct that component.

Inspect the closest published selection and adaptation methods, including the
remaining leads in the ledger, before selecting an experiment. The deliverable
should conclude that existing work answers the question, narrow it to an
identifying comparison, or explain why further investment is currently weak.
Workload design and execution would belong to a separately commissioned study.

## Selection after the methods review

The user asked the root to find and create at least two further ancillary studies.
The [completed reading](../sources/2026-09-17-independent-study-selection/README.md)
substantially narrows the first. Generic cross-model transfer, unchanged transfer
to an unseen recipient, model-aware sharing and even model-upgrade forecasting
already have direct precedents (P75, P78, P80, P84). Another demonstration that
memory can move between models would add little here. We select instead:

> Can paid experience with a previously unseen recipient improve the choice to
> retain, adapt or retire acquired procedural guidance enough to justify its
> cost, compared with competent unchanged inheritance and reconstruction?

This is **procedural-memory-migration**, an independent bounded investigation.
Model replacement changes the value of behavioral guidance even when the facts
and obligations remain fixed. Examples include a useful instruction about a
tool, a workaround for an old weakness, or advice that unnecessarily constrains
the new model. These are possible mechanisms, not local findings. The study
should establish functioning guidance before interpreting a migration failure.

Keep one acquired source bank and its underlying evidence fixed when isolating
the recipient effect. Assess inheritance against the recipient's own competent
behavior. A bank selected for a target model already present in its construction
history answers a different question from a previously unseen recipient. Give
identity-based methods a developed fallback; an empty unmatched bank would be
an artificially weak control. Begin with a model change; harness migration is
an optional extension if it distinguishes an additional explanation.

The useful comparison includes unchanged retrieval, the recipient without
inherited guidance, rebuilding from common source evidence, and a modest
recipient-informed adaptation or retirement rule. No factorial is prescribed.
The investigator chooses sufficient contrasts, workloads and methods. Preserve
ordinary tools, tests and feedback across alternatives. Inspection, adaptation,
validation, failed attempts and later use all incur costs. A post-hoc best action
or a prediction of migration accuracy is not a deployed selection policy.

The critical result concerns fresh complete tasks after calibration. A score
forecast can be accurate without changing the useful action; apparent adaptation
can just recover facts denied to a baseline. Let the simple policy, unchanged
bank or retirement win. Demonstrating when extra decisions fail to repay their
cost is useful progress.

## Prospective expectations — PM1–PM3

These are new expectations, recorded before ancillary execution. They do not
replace AD1–AD3, EU1–EU3 or EX1–EX3.

- **PM1:** The useful value of inherited procedural guidance will depend on the recipient; some guidance that helps its source may be redundant or harmful after a model change. Uniformly useful inheritance would weaken this expectation within the tested scope.
- **PM2:** Paid recipient experience will improve adaptation or retirement decisions only where it distinguishes consequential alternatives. A strong unchanged bank, simple rule or observation cost may remove its advantage.
- **PM3:** Access to the same source experience will make competent reconstruction a stronger competitor than adaptation from the inherited artifact alone. Selective adaptation can still repay its cost when useful guidance survives and reconstruction repeats substantial work.
