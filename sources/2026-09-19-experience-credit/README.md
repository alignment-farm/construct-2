# Experience credit and the value of checking a lesson — 19 September 2026

Root question: when does an additional check on a proposed reusable lesson improve
later work enough to repay its cost? This reading follows the accidental SQL
answer match in the [continuing-consolidation assessment](../../studies/2026-09-18-continuing-consolidation-findings.md)
and the memory-worker development authorized in Construct Runtime. It does not
review the derivative's work in progress or commission an experiment.

## New inspected papers

**P92 — [CHIME, 2609.02074v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02074v1), 2 September.**
Read §§3–6: method, setup, main comparisons and attribution analyses. A frozen
policy reflects on episodes, attributes outcomes to planning/execution/both/neither,
and selectively updates external memory. The authors report downstream gains and
an attribution-gate ablation. Repeated-attribution agreement is not causal truth;
same-episode rescue does not isolate improvement over ordinary retry or establish
future lesson value. The stronger-credit-model comparison also changes experience
generation. **Narrows:** reflective selection is a serious existing comparator;
the remaining question concerns the added value and cost of executed verification.

**P93 — [Causal Agent Replay, 2606.08275v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08275v1),
6 June.** Read §§2–5 and §7: intervention/replay method, synthetic validation and
limitations. It changes actions or observations and reruns continuations to estimate
outcome effects; interacting steps receive a Shapley treatment. Validation concerns
small constructed causal systems. The paper acknowledges downstream resampling,
expensive replay and limits with real side effects. **Redirects method selection:**
replay offers an intervention tool, but does not establish learning from resulting
lessons, future transfer or verification-cost repayment. An episode-level effect
alone does not establish the scope of a reusable rule.

## Root selection

Together with the prior feedback reading, these papers rule out presenting generic
credit assignment, memory ablation or reflection-before-storage as the next
contribution. The narrower candidate is **selective paid verification of a lesson
before reuse**, compared with retaining source episodes and with competent cheap
checks/reflection. Its evaluation must concern later complete work, including
unchanged obligations, and charge the checking itself.

The [root development note](../../notes/FEEDBACK_DECISIONS.md#checking-a-reusable-lesson--19-september)
separates candidate generation, acceptance and later use. Workload feasibility
remains unestablished. If existing checks or raw replay resolve the useful decision
cheaply, that would close this candidate without another learning experiment.
