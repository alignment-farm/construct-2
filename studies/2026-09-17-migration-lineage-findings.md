## Procedural migration: unchanged guidance is a strong alternative

The study holds a source experience archive and task obligations fixed while
changing the recipient from quantized Qwen3.8 27B to Qwen3 8B. This is a model
change, not an established capability upgrade. Three SQLite families require
independent aggregation, latest-event selection, and NULL-safe universal
exclusion. Every attempt receives its schema, obligations, public example and
ordinary SQL checking. It has four actions, including submission. Complete
success requires correct ordered results on all twelve hidden database fixtures;
those fixtures are checks on one attempt, not twelve independent model tasks.
Family identity and card retrieval are supplied by the harness.

**Acquisition was developed before migration.** Six source attempts, including
one failure, supply the common archive. The first acquired bank passes 6/6 source
screen tasks, as does no memory, but uses 30.3% more tokens and fails the preset
quality-or-efficiency gate. That failed gate remains in the evidence. A single
compact-card revision, built from the same original archive and frozen before a
fresh six-task screen, again passes 6/6 while reducing tokens by 41.2% and actions
from eleven to six. It changes both length and checking advice; different screen
tasks prevent attributing the improvement to compression alone. The source
fixture generator was also strengthened during initial acquisition; each saved
outcome is replayed with its own committed generator, and later comparisons use
the common strengthened generator.

The recipient gets unchanged compact cards, no memory, or cards reconstructed
from the identical original archive. A paid policy uses two calibration tasks
per family per alternative, selecting highest success then lowest token cost.
Frozen at `092ae7d` before final tasks, it chooses reconstruction for aggregation
and exclusion, and inheritance for temporal queries. Evaluation prospectively
reuses the selected static-arm outcomes; it is not a fourth independent run.

| Recipient policy | Complete fresh tasks | Use tokens | Incremental setup tokens | Setup + use tokens |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No inherited guidance | 1/12 | 49,590 | 0 | 49,590 |
| Unchanged inheritance | 12/12 | 28,153 | 0 | 28,153 |
| Reconstruct from shared archive | 10/12 | 23,925 | 11,086 | 35,011 |
| Paid family selection | 12/12 | 23,281 | 60,214 | 83,495 |

Selection avoids reconstruction's two temporal failures, but unchanged inheritance
already avoids them. It saves 4,872 use tokens, then spends 60,214 on reconstruction
and calibration. The resulting total is nearly three times unchanged inheritance.
Its roughly 149-use token break-even is a stationary extrapolation, not observed
repayment or a reliability forecast. Reconstruction's failed submissions retain
an unadapted table name; they do adapt the example cutoff. A stale numeric fact
is therefore not the supported explanation.

Matched final source controls are valuable: both source arms pass 12/12, and the
same inherited cards reduce source use tokens from 20,918 to 12,953. The recipient
instead rises from 1/12 without cards to 12/12 with them. The recipient changes
the guidance's marginal value, but no tested family becomes harmful or redundant
overall. The no-memory result describes this non-thinking model and bounded
tool protocol, not the best performance obtainable from every possible harness.

The table treats already-acquired source experience as sunk in the migration
decision. Actual historical source development costs 73,711 tokens, including
the failed first bank and both screens. The narrower fixed compact recipe costs
20,343 tokens for source experience and bank construction before qualification.
Neither is zero. The entire investigation records 216 calls, 108 task attempts
(89 successful, 19 failed), and 269,509 tokens including probes. Cross-model token
totals are accounting measures, not equivalent currency or compute; engineering
labor and model provisioning are unpriced. Measured shared-server latency does
not establish deployment speedup.

### Assessment of PM1–PM3

The [original predictions](../notes/EXPERIENCE_PORTABILITY.md#prospective-expectations--pm1pm3)
remain unchanged.

- **PM1 — mixed, bounded evidence.** Recipient-dependent marginal value is
  observed. The anticipated redundancy or harm is not: unchanged guidance helps
  both models. Uniform useful inheritance weakens that part of PM1 here.
- **PM2 — the limiting condition is observed.** Paid observations distinguish
  alternatives and yield a useful fixed choice on fresh tasks, but cannot improve
  quality over the strong unchanged bank and do not repay their cost at twelve
  uses. This is evidence against a net advantage for this selector and horizon.
- **PM3 — shared-archive reconstruction is tested, its causal advantage is not.**
  Reconstruction completes 10/12 and fails to beat inheritance. There is no
  archive-deprived or artifact-only adaptation contrast, so the predicted benefit
  of archive access and repayment by selective adaptation remain unestablished.

The phase closes on functioning acquisition, a consequential recipient contrast
and an explained cost limitation. One model pair, three supplied families and
twelve final tasks do not settle harness migration, long-run repayment, automatic
applicability or reliability under changing obligations.

## Correction lineage: fewer repaired fields need not mean less work

The recipient is fixed at quantized Qwen3.8 27B. Eight short source records support
eight dispatch-memory fields. Three fresh parameter instances each undergo two
cumulative authoritative corrections and subsequent orders for two new quantities.
The six episodes per arm are correlated within three instances, not six independent
workloads. A one-instance pilot precedes these fresh runs.

An actual model-written state is shared across four alternatives. Selective
lineage acquires semantic source links in a separate call and pays to maintain
them after repair. Exposure lineage conservatively records all visible inputs.
Reconstruction infers affected keys when correction arrives. Rebuilding regenerates
the full state. Repairs and subsequent tasks have the same original/current
source archive and corrections, with source-checking instructions. Evaluator
support labels and state truth are excluded from requests. All methods can thus
check the evidence rather than blindly trust memory.

| Method, three fresh histories | Complete repaired states | Complete state + later orders | Recomputed fields | Deployment calls | Deployment tokens |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Acquired selective lineage | 6/6 | 4/6 | 18 | 24 | 13,613 |
| Conservative exposure | 6/6 | 4/6 | 48 | 15 | 10,017 |
| Reconstruct dependencies | 6/6 | 4/6 | 48 | 21 | 14,037 |
| Rebuild whole state | 6/6 | 4/6 | 48 | 15 | 9,789 |

Costs include initial writes, acquisition where applicable, correction, lineage
maintenance and later audited use. Shared experimental writes are charged once
to each hypothetical deployment, not repeatedly per episode. Selective lineage
recomputes fewer fields but costs 39.1% more tokens than rebuilding. The complete
archive fits every repair prompt, so field selectivity saves little input work
while separate acquisition and maintenance calls add overhead.

There is a small **observed crossover against dependency reconstruction**:
after one correction and use, selective lineage costs 8,276 tokens versus 7,736;
after two, 13,613 versus 14,037. Rebuilding remains cheaper at both horizons
(5,603 and 9,789). Beating one comparison does not establish an allocation
advantage over the strongest tested alternative. Joint state/link generation,
larger archives and longer histories were not tested.

**Acquisition succeeds, and maintenance introduces a distinct error.** Initial
links match all 33 fresh evaluator support candidates, with no extras or omissions.
Those labels cover all authoritative functional inputs, including independently
sufficient escort requirements; they are not a unique minimal causal explanation.
A spurious link to an unapproved route suggestion appears after maintenance in
one of three fresh instances, as anticipated after the pilot. Neither scheduled
correction changes that suggestion, so the false edge does not cause an observed
repair failure. All arms correctly preserve the site escort requirement when
the account requirement is removed, then remove escort after the site correction.
There is no demonstrated selective-lineage advantage on preservation.

**The complete-task failures occur after correct repair.** For each seed/event,
later-use requests and parsed outputs are identical across the four arms. Two
first-correction episodes produce wrong order totals despite correct persistent
state; this cannot explain a quality difference between maintenance methods.
The use instruction's arithmetic wording is a plausible contributor. A separately
frozen diagnostic on two new instances compares the original wording with an
explicit formula: original succeeds 1/2, explicit 2/2 with the same repaired state
and evidence. Only one pair distinguishes the prompts. This narrows the read-time
explanation without proving a general cure or retrospectively replacing 4/6.

Actual research totals are 97 calls and 63,575 tokens: 88 pilot/fresh calls,
one capability probe and eight separate diagnostic calls. Time, token accounting
and field counts do not include all labor or hardware cost. The fixed flat schema,
short histories, supplied authority and shared audited reader bound the result.

### Assessment of CL1–CL3

The [original predictions](../notes/CORRECTION_LINEAGE.md#prospective-expectations--cl1cl3)
remain unchanged.

- **CL1 — partial support and an untested failure threshold.** Accurate initial
  acquisition enables selective repair; later false support demonstrates that
  maintaining links is itself fallible. No missed correction or task loss is
  caused by that error here, and reduced field count does not establish useful
  savings. Reliability sufficient for long-lived selective repair is unresolved.
- **CL2 — bounded cost evidence.** Reconstructing dependencies is initially
  cheaper, and selective maintenance passes it after the second correction.
  Whole-state rebuilding remains cheapest. The small-archive setting favors
  inexpensive reconstruction of state, without establishing a general crossover.
- **CL3 — preservation succeeds across all alternatives.** The independent
  support contrast matters to the task and is handled correctly by every method.
  The predicted advantage over aggressive invalidation is not directly tested.
  Rebuilding matches tested quality at lower cost, while later arithmetic exposes
  why repaired state alone is an insufficient outcome.

The phase closes on functioning acquisition/repair, an explained cost result,
and fresh diagnostic progress on the shared later-use failure. It does not close
the broader value of lineage under expensive reconstruction or longer histories.

## What changes at root

Useful experience survives a changed recipient in the first study. Correct
state survives authoritative corrections in the second. These are positive
capability results. Both also show that **the machinery for choosing or maintaining
memory must justify its own work**. Better per-use efficiency and fewer repaired
fields can coexist with higher total cost. Their causes differ; the studies do
not establish a common internal failure or a universal preference for rebuilding.

Together with [executable retention](2026-09-17-executable-retention-findings.md),
the comparisons strengthen a practical inference: preserve recoverable evidence
and useful artifacts, then compare added adaptation with competent reuse and
reconstruction over the relevant lifetime. An archive is a shared enabling
condition here; neither new study isolates the causal value of retaining it.
The fixed hybrid hypothesis is sharpened, not newly discovered or fully validated.

The [public follow-up](../sources/2026-09-17-migration-lineage-review/README.md)
revisits migration forecasting and source-grounded behavior repair, and adds
MemLineage's distinction between attribution, enforcement and successful recovery.
It narrows what a future comparison must explain; it supplies no reason to repeat
these small experiments automatically.

Thirteen registered studies now have accepted bounded contributions; no current
commission remains in execution. The root's next evidential milestone remains
continuing useful capability on a realistic job, with repeated use and consequential
change. Before commissioning more runs, theory and public methods should identify
a setting where the allocation decision matters beyond supplied family labels,
short fully visible archives and fixed repair routines. Changing sample size or
extending the latest method until it wins is not that selection argument.
AD1 remains untested: neither study learns to distinguish missing evidence from
failed use and select the appropriate remedy. Existing AD2/AD3 assessments stand.
