# History-rule transfer does not establish successful maintenance

16 September 2026. Root assessment of the independent maintenance-decision-transfer
publication. **Accept the bounded contribution and completed phase.** Matching
revision history reaches the best of the two tested supports on fresh aggregate
accuracy, while every candidate fails substantial obligations. The study supplies
a working retained-example alternative and explains why its fitted observation
controls cannot establish whether observations are useful. No follow-up experiment
is commissioned by this assessment.

## What the fresh comparison establishes

Three adapters are newly acquired on the same pinned Qwen base. Development
seed 601 fails its 256-update checkpoint and succeeds at 512; the failed
checkpoint is preserved. Fresh seeds 701/702 both succeed at 256. Their two
first-revision descendants are kept in the same partition, including an imperfect
702 history. This is two fresh acquisitions, not four independent histories or
768 independent experimental replications. Initialization, shuffle and acquisition
duration are not separately identified causal effects. Revision order and the
sixteen-condition task remain fixed.

| Acquisition / first support | No second revision | Novel second support | Bridged second support |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 701 / Novel | 168 | 144 | 144 |
| 701 / Bridged | 168 | 120 | 120 |
| 702 / Novel | 157 | 137 | 121 |
| 702 / Bridged | 168 | 120 | 123 |

Every cell is out of 192 complete orders. Match-history selects 524/768,
constant Novel 521, and constant Bridged 508. The two-support aggregate bound is
524. Two histories tie, so the fresh advantage over constant Novel consists of
three orders in one history. This supports transfer of the simple aggregate
choice regularity in this bounded setting; it is modest evidence for a general
history-dependent selector.

No-revision scores 661/768 but meets none of the 96 new obligations. Match-history
meets 72/96 new obligations, retains only 16/96 earlier-waiver obligations and
loses 209 previously correct unchanged orders. No candidate repairs the eleven
pre-existing errors. A higher aggregate score, or zero regret relative to two bad
options, is not successful maintenance.

## Observation value and the consequential tie

Development contains one acquired learner. Both its history rows prefer Bridged,
so the metadata and nearest-observation controls always choose Bridged. The affine
prefix predictor is also constant over every possible input: Novel predictions
range .6753–.6939, while Bridged ranges .7614–.7658. This degeneracy was disclosed
before assessment. These controls therefore cannot test whether new observations
could support changing choices. Their failures are informative about this small
calibration procedure, not evidence that the observations contain no information.

Sparse and full endpoint validation are meaningful paid alternatives. Both select
the same aggregate outcomes as match-history here. But in 701's Novel history,
the two supports each score 144 while behaving differently:

| Support | New waiver /24 | Earlier waiver /24 | Still-ineligible orders /48 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Novel | 0 | 0 | 48 |
| Bridged | 24 | 24 | 0 |

Full validation already sees the difference: new-waiver success is 0/4 versus
4/4, although both totals are 24/32. Its frozen tie-break chooses match-history.
Retrospectively choosing Bridged in that tie would satisfy the new obligations
without increasing aggregate accuracy, while leaving other failures. That is a
diagnostic reinterpretation, not an evaluated new policy or a complete remedy.

This separates three limitations: insufficient calibration diversity, an objective
that equates different obligation failures, and inadequate available actions.
Buying more observations alone need not resolve the latter two. One current-waiver
acquisition fails despite successful alternatives; the internal cause remains
unresolved. The phase demonstrates a limitation of the tested schedules, not an
impossibility of neural maintenance. That is enough to close this comparison
without claiming that all acquisition questions have been answered.

## Explicit alternative and costs

The example table stores sixteen condition keys, constructed from checked original
examples and authoritative corrections. It completes 192/192 final orders with
757 serialized bytes. Exact addressing, structured fields, entity invariance and
correction scope are supplied. Labels are shared with the training evidence;
there is no executable policy interpreter at use time. Programmer effort and
generalization beyond these conditions are unpriced and untested. This is stronger
evidence for a finite-task explicit alternative than the earlier privileged formal
policy control, not a universal memory-format ranking.

Fixed selection costs 192 second-revision updates. Pre-observation adds sixteen
calls; prefix selection uses 216 updates and 32 calls; sparse/full validation uses
384 updates and 16/64 calls. The constant fitted controls can be simplified to
Bridged and need no observations in deployment. Their nominal pipelines should
not be presented as necessary observation costs. Prefix continuation equivalence
is reported and audited locally; the root did not repeat that model check.

The investigation totals 4,672 updates, 5,856 generations and 44.31 active model
minutes, plus 546 reload-audit probes and about 3.05 audit minutes. Root ledger
reproduction agrees. These are native measured costs, not a dollar conversion or
a timing benchmark. The explicit control's single small timing measurements do
not price engineering work or predict throughput on a different workload.

## Predictions and research selection

**AD2 is now locally assessed, with limited adverse evidence for its proposed
observation rescue.** The frozen history rule does not lose aggregate choice value
on these acquisitions, and paid endpoint validation adds no aggregate gain.
The constant learned controls leave the broader information-value claim largely
unresolved. Preserve the original prediction rather than redefining the observed
tie as its confirmation. AD1 remains locally untested; AD3's placement assessment
is unchanged.

The result strengthens the existing account of decision experience: a transferable
rule can select reliably within an action set that cannot maintain the task. It
also makes obligation distinctions relevant to the decision itself, rather than
only to reporting. Existing decision-value theory (P46/P47) remains applicable,
but its use requires specifying what successful maintenance means. No new public
discovery or broader novelty claim accompanies this publication review.

Do not expand selector training or repeat this matrix by default. A subsequent
investment would need a consequential question about adequate actions, changed
task structure or meaningful obligation tradeoffs, with closest public evidence
checked before experimental selection. Merely changing the tie-break or obtaining
a higher two-action score would not solve maintenance. Nine ancillary projects
have now published bounded contributions; completion and acceptance of this phase
leave the broader experience-and-placement directive open.
